**NARRATOR** – Through the same gap in the fence, you make your way back into the inn's backyard. The rain has stopped, leaving behind sucking mud and air thick with the stench of rot, as though it had been left to steep. The two red-haired children are hanging around by the shed, quietly talking among themselves.

His face, by contrast, radiates good humor and complete satisfaction with life.

**VERNON ROCHE** – Well, Captain Meis kept his word.

**FORTITUDE** [Challenging: Failure] – The stench is unbearable. You'll never wash it off. I don't envy the coroner.

**VERNON ROCHE** – ...The juvenile offender, having entered the crime scene without authorization, admitted to mutilating the body by throwing stones at it. He also stated that the rocks were going fucking fast.

**RUNE** – Damn right!

**KEEN EYE** – Look at the hands bound behind his back.

**YOU** – His hands were tied behind his back at the wrists. Might be worth taking a closer look.

**NARRATOR** – Vernon gives the guard with the saw a curt nod. Fighting down his disgust, the man reluctantly starts sawing through the rope.

**RUNA** – Now the stiff's gonna clap his hands!

**NARRATOR** – The guard snorts through his nose, doing his best to suppress a gag. The rope fibers snap apart. You lean in closer.

**KEEN EYE** – There are clear marks on the wrists. But they're strange: no abrasions, no bruising, none of the dark, ragged lines you'd expect if a living person had struggled against the bindings. Just deep, even grooves left by the rope.

**DIALECTIC** [Challenging: Success] – If he'd been tied up while still alive, you'd expect blood beneath the skin, chafing, signs of a struggle. There's none of that here.

**YOU** – Vernon... Looks like his hands were tied after he was already dead.

**NARRATOR** – Roche stops writing and looks at you over the top of his notebook.

**VERNON ROCHE** – What makes you think that?

**YOU** – The rope marks are there, but they're just indentations. He doesn't seem to have struggled against the bindings.

**RUNE** – Get this — some freak tied him up after he was already dead! That's fucked up!

**VERNON ROCHE** – That is... highly unusual. I think we should wait for the coroner to confirm it. But if you're right, when could the binding have taken place? Hardly after he was already dangling well above the ground...

**NARRATOR** – You pause, thinking it over.

**DIALECTIC** [Easy: Failure] – Yes. The perfect moment for a dramatic logical deduction... So, what we have here is... Hmm. Hm-m.

**YOU** – What's wrong?

**DIALECTIC** – Hm? What? Nothing. Everything's fine.

**DIALECTIC** – The deduction? What deduction?

**YOU** – Brilliant...

**VERNON ROCHE** – But if his hands were tied earlier, then... they hanged the body after he was already dead?..

**NARRATOR** – He looks at you in bewilderment. Even the children on the roof have fallen quiet. Somewhere in the distance, gulls are crying.

**VERNON ROCHE** – What would be the point? Was someone trying to mislead the investigation and conceal the true cause or place of death?

**NARRATOR** – He's mostly talking to himself now.

**VERNON ROCHE** – If so, that only makes the case more complicated — assuming, of course, that our conclusions are even correct. Still, we shouldn't get ahead of ourselves. Let's wait for the coroner's opinion.

**YOU** – Yes... probably.

**ARTISTRY** [Easy: Success] – The sort who beg for alms outside the Temple of the Eternal Fire, wailing the same pitiful story year after year.

**GUARD WITH THE LADDER** – Good day, Mr. Coroner! Let us give you a hand.

Take the poor fellow over yonder to the fence; my cart's waiting in the street. Easy with him, mind... He won't be telling *you* anything now, but he may yet have a thing or two to tell me. If you know how to ask the right questions, heh-heh...

**NARRATOR** – The guards salute, pick up the body with obvious disgust, lay it on the stretcher, and carry their mournful burden away from the vacant lot. Vernon watches them go, still lost in thought. The coroner wipes his forehead with a handkerchief. It was white once. A very long time ago.

**CORONER** – Well then, good sirs, I'll have a little report drawn up for you by tomorrow and send it over with a messenger straight away. Won't manage it today — I've already got more patients than I know what to do with.

**VERNON ROCHE** – I suggest we call it a day and refrain from any further theorizing until we receive the full report. Though I must admit, the investigation is taking an unexpected turn. It'll be dark soon... I think we should head back to the tavern and see about lodging for the night.

In the Merchant Quarter, you're more likely to find clerks, accountants, notaries, and other respectable functionaries with clean collars and dirty thoughts.
